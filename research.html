Notes

What each one was actually about

Netflix (Parts A & B): a full SWOT of Netflix as a global streaming platform, covering strengths like global reach and recommendation algorithms, weaknesses like rising content costs, and threats from Disney+ and Amazon Prime, followed by a ranked breakdown of its critical business challenges and a proposed hybrid pricing and content strategy.

Arm & Hammer: how a 170+ year old product sustained relevance through brand extension and packaging innovation, with e-commerce and B2B industrial cleaning proposed as new growth paths.

Peloton: how the brand segments and targets tech-savvy, fitness-conscious earners, and whether its premium lifestyle positioning holds up beyond fitness equipment alone.

EpiPen: the Mylan pricing controversy, with proposed solutions including price transparency, non-profit manufacturing models, and regulatory reform.

McDonald's: iMotions eye-tracking research comparing emotion-forward and product-forward digital ads, which surfaced the Vampire Effect (emotional creative overshadowing the product itself) and led to a hybrid-strategy recommendation.

Walmart: a shelf planogram redesign using eye-level focus, brand blocking between Finish and Cascade, and cross-sell placement, projected to drive a $92 lift in total dollars from repositioning additive SKUs.

Vadilal Industries: a segmentation and campaign analysis project run across 20+ Google and Meta campaigns for an established FMCG brand, where the real gain came from getting more precise about targeting, not from spending more.
