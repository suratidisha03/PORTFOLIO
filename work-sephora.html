01 / Problem

Skincare shoppers buy on trust: will it work for my skin, will it feel right, is it worth the price. Reviews hold those doubts in the shopper's own words, but they rarely reach the page copy, the emails or the campaign decisions. I set myself one question and followed it through the whole journey: search, product page, tracking, email, repeat purchase and reporting.

This is a research question I proposed. It does not claim Sephora has a problem.

03 / Method

Cleaned the reviews in SQLite with logged checks for row counts, duplicates and joins. Coded 150 recent reviews by theme, checked 20 by hand (70% agreement, 6 codes corrected), then confirmed each theme across all 9,999 reviews in SQL. Turned the themes into copy for a landing page, product pages, an SEO article, 7 emails and 2 ad hooks, each line traced to a review theme or a verified product fact.

Built a demo site and tracked 6 ecommerce events with GTM and GA4, verified in Tag Assistant and DebugView. Simulated 8 linked tables and locked 11 metric definitions before running any analysis, then wrote 6 SQL analyses: data QA, campaigns, funnel, customers and RFM, cohorts and email. Designed 3 lifecycle flows with entry, exit and suppression rules, tested them on 10 practice contacts in HubSpot Free, and built and test-sent all 7 emails in Mailchimp. Finally built 2 dashboards and checked every total against SQL.

05 / Finding & Decision
What I Found

Pilling complaints on the cream are growing, and part of it happens only when layered over other products.

Scent decides undecided 3-star buyers of the cleanser.

254 one-time lapsed and 125 at-risk buyers are the largest lifecycle audiences.

↗
What I'd Do

Lead the cream page and the day-2 welcome email with a layering guide.

Describe the scent and lather plainly and offer the mini first.

Point the win-back flow at both groups, capped at 1 email a day and 3 a week.

07 / Validation

53 logged QA checks cover data loads, joins, duplicates, metric reconciliation, tracking, email rendering and dashboards. Money reconciles: $6,267 attributed + $228 lost-tag + $76,002 non-paid = $82,497 total. Funnel steps count only in time order within one session, and cohort months not yet reached stay blank, never 0. Both dashboards match SQL and open signed out.

Errors I caught and fixed: the first cart email linked to an empty bag, so I made the page rebuild the bag from the link and retested it from my inbox on the live site. Future cohort months showed as shaded cells in Tableau, so I rebuilt the chart from a file without them. AI-coded reviews were checked by hand before use, and all AI use is logged.

09 / Limitations

Performance data is simulated: it proves I can calculate and explain the metrics, not that these campaigns or emails would work. Several patterns were built into the inputs (search vs social conversion, the repeat rate, the month-2 peak), and I say so wherever they appear. The reviews are a self-selected 2016 to 2023 sample with no sales, returns or skin outcomes. The lifecycle flows are prototypes: Mailchimp Free has no multi-step automation, so the emails were built and test-sent, not run as live flows. Tracking was tested on my own demo traffic only.

Next tests: A/B the layering guide on the cream page (add to cart), the scent copy (mini selection) and cost-per-week framing in the welcome email (click rate).
