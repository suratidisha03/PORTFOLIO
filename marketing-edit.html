What e.l.f. actually bought when it bought Rhode

In August 2025, e.l.f. Beauty closed a $1B acquisition of Rhode, Hailey Bieber's skincare brand, up to $800M at close plus a $200M earnout tied to growth. On paper it's a beauty deal. I don't think that's what it actually was.

Rhode was the number one skincare brand by earned media value in 2024, growing EMV 367% year over year, almost entirely without traditional advertising. e.l.f.'s own engine (community-led, disruption-first, built on cheap digital attention) had been getting more expensive to run every year. What it bought wasn't a supply chain or a product line. It was a way of getting unpaid attention at scale, already attached to a person instead of a product, at a moment when that attention was getting harder for anyone to manufacture from scratch.

The synergy is real, too: 75% of e.l.f.'s products sit under $10, which gives mass-market shoppers a cheap entry point into a $30–50 Rhode routine. Rhode's first Sephora North America launch broke retailer records, and Rhode alone added $113M to e.l.f.'s Q4 net sales growth. This is what buying earned media actually looks like on a balance sheet.

Beauty · Casting

Rare Beauty's first-ever ambassador, six years in

Rare Beauty launched without a celebrity ambassador and stayed that way for six years: a deliberate choice for a brand built around anti-perfectionism. In July 2026, it named Ella Bright, the breakout star of Off Campus, as its first-ever ambassador for the "Made You Blush" campaign. The casting isn't renting a famous face. It's recognizing a cultural moment early enough to attach itself before it cools.
