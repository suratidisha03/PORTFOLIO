Conjoint Analysis / Perceptual Mapping / Brand Strategy

Where does Pixel sit relative to competitors?

Academic research · DePaul University, MS Marketing Analytics

35 respondents · $800 recommended price point · 25.6% projected market share.

Role: Designed the conjoint study, built the perceptual map, and translated the analysis into a pricing and positioning recommendation.

01 / Marketing Question

What would make consumers choose Pixel over Apple, Samsung, Motorola, or OnePlus?

02 / Context

Google Pixel competes in a category where consumers already have strong, established preferences for Apple and Samsung. This academic research project set out to identify where Pixel had room to win: which product attributes actually shifted preference, and where it sat in consumers' minds relative to its four closest competitors.

03 / Data & Inputs

35 survey respondents completed a conjoint exercise rating smartphone attribute combinations (including price, camera quality, brand, and features), plus a perceptual-mapping survey positioning Pixel, Apple, Samsung, Motorola, and OnePlus against each other.

04 / Approach

Conjoint analysis isolated which attributes carried the most weight in respondents' choices. Perceptual mapping placed all five brands on the resulting dimensions, showing where Pixel was perceived as strong, and where the competitive gap was widest. A price-scenario test then modeled how a recommended price point would affect projected preference share.

Pixel's clearest opening was on value and camera quality, not brand prestige.

The conjoint results showed price and camera performance carrying more weight in respondents' choices than brand prestige, an attribute where Apple and Samsung dominate. The perceptual map confirmed the same story: Pixel already sat closer to "smart value" than "premium status" in respondents' minds, which meant leaning into that space had more upside than trying to out-prestige Apple.

06 / What That Meant for Marketing

Recommended positioning Pixel around camera performance and value rather than competing directly on brand prestige, and recommended an $800 price point as the scenario that best balanced perceived value against premium credibility in the model.

07 / Recommendation & Impact

A projected market share of 25.6% at the modeled $800 price point. This is a projected recommendation from the conjoint model, not an observed business result.

09 / What the Data Said

Price and camera performance carried more weight in respondents' choices than brand prestige, and the perceptual map placed Pixel closer to "smart value" than "premium status." At the modeled $800 price point, the conjoint output projected a 25.6% preference share, evidence that Pixel's clearest opening is value and camera quality, not competing on prestige.

10 / Reflection

Re-run the conjoint with a larger, more geographically representative sample to check whether the value and camera preference holds across different consumer segments before treating the $800 recommendation as final.
