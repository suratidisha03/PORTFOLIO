01 / Problem

Oatly's North America revenue fell 9.1% in 2025 to $249.6M after it lost sales to its largest café customer. By Q3 2026 about 68% of North America revenue came from retail, up from 61%. Growth now has to come from people's kitchens, not café contracts.

The people most likely to buy are the ones already ordering oat lattes at cafés. They like the drink. The question is why they don't make it at home.

02 / Research

I listened first, then cross-checked. I coded 85 public comments from US home coffee drinkers on Reddit, TikTok and YouTube by their main barrier, then checked the pattern against 80 Target reviews of four barista oat milks and five years of Google Trends.

The same three problems came up again and again: taste and texture, not knowing how to do it, and foam. 26 of 29 negative Target reviews said the same thing: my home oat latte disappoints and I don't know how to fix it.

03 / Insight & Idea

Insight: "I love my oat latte. I just can't make one at home that doesn't taste sad." On TikTok, the top results for "oat milk won't froth" are home baristas asking for help. No oat milk brand answers them.

Idea: Make Every Pour Count. Cafés are great, and your kitchen can make a good latte too, with the right carton and a simple method. An extra occasion, not a replacement. The voice borrows from the carton itself: plain, honest and a little absurd.

Every ad has one job in the funnel, and every job leads to one main metric: a completed search on Oatly's store finder.

05 / CRM & Organic

Emails that branch on behaviour. Sign-ups get a welcome email, a "foam fixes" email on day 3 and a reminder on day 7. Anyone who clicks a recipe moves into a three-email Recipe Club instead of the generic reminder. Anyone who completes a store search exits the flow. The copy never blames milk in general, only the wrong carton.

08 / Finding & Decision
What I Found (simulated)

Retargeting wore out in week 7: its last $989 bought 32 searches, $30.90 each.

The how-to message was 15% cheaper per landing-page view than the taste message.

Compared fairly (same funnel stage), the how-to track cost $8.96 per search vs $9.81 for the result track.

↗
What I'd Do

Pause retargeting when the rule fires and move the money to the carousel: about 120 searches instead of 32, an upper bound since costs rise with spend.

Shift AD-02 budget to the how-to version and use the method angle in new creative.

Lead the next round of Reels with the black-card hook.

09 / Limitations

All performance data is simulated: the results reflect my assumptions, including the edge I gave message B, so they prove the method, not the creative. The research is a small, hand-collected sample that leans toward espresso hobbyists. A completed store search shows intent, not a purchase. Visuals use stock photography and animation in place of a shoot. The Latte Math price comes from one Target listing on one day.

With a real budget I'd shoot creator-style video, run a city-based holdout test against retail sales, scale Foam Fixes with paid coffee creators, and test the email 2 variant.
